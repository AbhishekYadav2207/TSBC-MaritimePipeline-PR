\section{Benchmark Results}
\label{sec:results}

The benchmark evaluates whether measured masked-token prediction performance depends on model choice, the way maritime records are represented as text, or the informativeness of the evaluated subset. It compares seven pretrained encoders under five representations and five subsets, yielding 175 matched configurations. Because every encoder is evaluated under the same representation--subset conditions, the resulting comparisons help distinguish consistent model differences from effects associated with the input format or evaluation subset. The results are presented in this order: representation effects, encoder performance, tokenizer behaviour, statistical evidence, diagnostic masking results, and the resulting initialization decision.

\subsection{Representation and Subset Effects}
\label{sec:repr}
\input{tables/table3_marginals}

A maritime occurrence can be expressed as continuous narrative, a set of key--value fields, a standardized template, a JSON object, or a mixture of structured fields and prose. Although these representations describe the same underlying records, they expose information differently to a masked language model. Narrative text provides surrounding linguistic context, whereas structured formats make field boundaries and attribute names more explicit. Consequently, changing the representation can alter the difficulty of predicting a masked token even when the underlying record has not changed.

The results show that this formatting choice has a substantial effect on measured performance. Mean Top-1 accuracy varies by 25.09 percentage points across representations, compared with 3.98 points between the most and least favourable informativeness subsets. The representation-related spread is therefore 6.30 times the subset-related spread. In the evaluated cohort, narrative and mixed representations generally provide more favourable prediction contexts than JSON and key--value forms (Table~\ref{tab:marginals}; Figure~\ref{fig:repr_tok}a).

The practical implication is that an encoder score cannot be interpreted independently of how its input was serialized. Comparing a score obtained from narrative text with one obtained from JSON would mix a model comparison with a formatting change. For this reason, the leaderboard should be read alongside the representation-specific results, and differences between encoders should be interpreted primarily under matched conditions.

\subsection{Encoder Ranking and Robustness}
\label{sec:ranking}
\input{tables/table4_leaderboard}

Across the primary benchmark, ModernBERT achieves the highest mean maritime Top-1 accuracy at 57.99\% across its 25 matched conditions. Top-1 accuracy measures how often the correct masked token is ranked as the model's first prediction. RoBERTa is the closest competitor, with a mean of 46.12\%. ModernBERT also records a MECS of 87.40, with a Top-1 accuracy of 58.14\% in the JSON $\times$ Balanced condition (Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}). The aggregate result favours ModernBERT, while the individual condition illustrates why model-level averages and particular configurations should not be treated as interchangeable.

The comparison with RoBERTa is especially informative because both models are evaluated over the same 25 conditions. ModernBERT leads in 20 conditions, with RoBERTa narrowly ahead only for Narrative (45.16\% versus 45.44\%). The matched-condition bootstrap interval for ModernBERT is $52.81, 63.42$, compared with $40.92, 51.01$ for RoBERTa; the reported intervals remain separated. The average advantage is 11.87 percentage points. Together, these results indicate a consistent overall advantage under the primary protocol, without implying that ModernBERT wins under every representation--subset combination.

The other encoders illustrate why a single ranking is not sufficient for every operational decision. The multi-criteria Pareto analysis identifies ModernBERT, RoBERTa, SciBERT, BERT-base, and Legal-BERT as non-dominated. In this analysis, a model is non-dominated when no other evaluated model is at least as good across every selected objective and strictly better on at least one. The result therefore preserves meaningful alternatives when predictive performance must be balanced against resource use or tokenizer behaviour. BERT-base has 110M parameters and a measured latency of 19.51\,ms, while SciBERT and Legal-BERT provide different non-dominated vocabulary trade-offs. BioBERT and PubMedBERT are strictly dominated within the objectives and cohort considered here.

The lowest aggregate score belongs to PubMedBERT at 20.29\%, and the observed spread across encoders is 37.70 percentage points, from ModernBERT at 57.99\% to PubMedBERT at 20.29\%. These results describe this particular set of checkpoints under this protocol; they do not establish that specialized pretraining is inherently inferior. The encoders differ in pretraining data, scale, architecture, tokenizer, and training history, so the comparison is observational rather than a controlled causal test of domain specialization.

\subsection{Tokenizer Coverage and Tokenization Dynamics}
\label{sec:tokenizer}

Vocabulary coverage helps explain why a model with a tokenizer that fits the terminology well does not necessarily achieve the strongest contextual predictions. Single-token coverage measures the proportion of evaluated terms represented by one token rather than being split into multiple subword pieces. BERT-base has the highest single-token coverage at 73.43\%, yet ranks fourth in mean Top-1 accuracy. ModernBERT leads the primary benchmark despite greater fragmentation (Figure~\ref{fig:repr_tok}b).

This distinction matters because compact tokenization and accurate contextual prediction measure different properties. A tokenizer may encode a maritime term as a single unit, but the model can still assign a lower rank to that token in context. Another encoder may divide the same term into multiple pieces while predicting those pieces effectively from the surrounding text. Top-1 accuracy therefore reflects both segmentation and the encoder's contextual predictions; it should not be read as a tokenizer-independent measure of domain knowledge.

Taken together, the results favour assessing vocabulary coverage, fragmentation, and contextual recovery jointly rather than selecting a model from vocabulary statistics alone. Under the primary random-masking protocol, ModernBERT provides the strongest aggregate recovery performance despite its higher fragmentation. This conclusion remains limited to the evaluated encoders and protocol, since alternative masking strategies or downstream tasks may produce different trade-offs.

\subsection{Crossed Factorial Statistical Validation}
\label{sec:stats}

The statistical analysis asks whether the differences seen in the tables persist across the matched evaluation design, rather than being driven by one favourable condition. The crossed repeated-measures analysis separates the contributions associated with model identity, representation, subset, and their pairwise interactions. Its most important result is that both the encoder and the representation matter, and that the effect of representation varies by encoder.

Model identity accounts for 55.66\% of the total variance, while representation accounts for 25.47\%. The Model $\times$ Representation interaction accounts for a further 15.34\%, showing that serialization does not affect all encoders in the same way. By comparison, Subset accounts for 0.71\% of the variance. The Model $\times$ Subset and Representation $\times$ Subset interactions account for 0.59\% and 1.12\%, respectively, while the residual pooled three-way interaction accounts for 1.11\%. Thus, informativeness subset differences are detectable, but they are smaller contributors to variation than model identity and representation in this benchmark.

These patterns are supported by the crossed repeated-measures ANOVA: Model, $F(6,96)=804.16$, $p<10^{-15}$, $\eta_p^2=0.9805$; Representation, $F(4,96)=552.03$, $p<10^{-15}$, $\eta_p^2=0.9583$; Model $\times$ Representation, $F(24,96)=55.40$, $p<10^{-15}$, $\eta_p^2=0.9327$; Representation $\times$ Subset, $F(16,96)=6.06$, $p=5.25\times10^{-9}$, $\eta_p^2=0.5026$; Subset, $F(4,96)=15.47$, $p=8.46\times10^{-10}$, $\eta_p^2=0.3919$; and Model $\times$ Subset, $F(24,96)=2.12$, $p=0.00533$, $\eta_p^2=0.3469$. The partial eta-squared values describe effect magnitude within the fitted analysis; the variance contributions above provide the more direct comparison of each factor's share of total variation. The residual three-way interaction also cautions against extending the findings beyond the combinations evaluated here.

A Friedman omnibus test provides a complementary rank-based check across the 25 matched conditions. It confirms that the seven encoders do not perform equivalently across this benchmark ($\chi^2=121.17$, $\mathrm{df}=6$, $p=9.27\times10^{-24}$, Kendall's $W=0.8078$). Kendall's $W$ indicates strong agreement in the model rankings across conditions. The test establishes an overall difference among encoders; the pairwise analysis is needed to determine which model comparisons account for that difference.

\input{tables/table5_pairwise}

The pairwise results identify ModernBERT's advantage over RoBERTa as statistically supported after Holm correction for multiple comparisons. ModernBERT leads by 11.87 percentage points, with a 95\% confidence interval of $6.44, 17.30$, an adjusted $p$-value of $2.29\times10^{-4}$, paired $d_z=0.9026$, and 20 wins among the 25 matched conditions. The reported paired rank-biserial correlation is $r_{\mathrm{pb}}=0.8585$, while Cliff's $\delta=0.5104$ summarizes a separate unpaired comparison and should not be interpreted as the matched-condition win rate. Against the other encoders, ModernBERT wins at least 24 of 25 conditions, with $r_{\mathrm{pb}}\ge0.99$ and $\delta\ge0.83$ as reported.

Nineteen of the 21 pairwise comparisons survive Holm correction. SciBERT--Legal-BERT ($\Delta=+4.42$ percentage points, $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=0.170$) and BERT-base--Legal-BERT ($\Delta=+0.11$ percentage points, $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=0.692$) are not statistically distinguishable after adjustment. This does not demonstrate that either pair is equivalent; it means the analysis does not establish a difference between them at the chosen correction threshold. Overall, the inferential evidence supports ModernBERT as the leading initialization within the evaluated cohort, while leaving the model- and format-specific scope of the conclusions explicit.

\subsection{Masking Sensitivity and Pseudo-Log-Likelihood}
\label{sec:masking}
\input{tables/table6_masking}

The primary benchmark uses random masking of 15\% of subword positions. This protocol provides the main basis for the leaderboard above. A separate domain-aware masking diagnostic, DA-15, keeps the 15\% masking budget but preferentially selects technical terms. It asks a different question: how well can each model recover terms deliberately targeted for their domain relevance, rather than masked subwords sampled under the primary protocol?

Under DA-15, Top-1 accuracy decreases across all models. The DA-Rare condition produces a particularly notable change in the ordering: RoBERTa reaches 53.23\% and SciBERT 38.71\%, both exceeding ModernBERT at 8.01\%. This result does not negate ModernBERT's lead in the primary benchmark; it reveals that aggregate random-masking performance does not necessarily predict performance when the evaluation concentrates on rare technical vocabulary. The rare-term condition should therefore be interpreted as a targeted stress test, not as a replacement leaderboard or a directly comparable estimate of general masked-token performance.

Sampled pseudo-log-likelihood provides a second, auxiliary view of model behaviour. Unlike Top-1 accuracy, which records whether the correct token is the highest-ranked prediction at a masked position, pseudo-log-likelihood evaluates token-level conditional likelihoods over sampled text. The corresponding sampled pseudo-perplexity is 13.72 for ModernBERT, 16.02 for RoBERTa, 25.50 for BERT-base, and 91.56 for PubMedBERT. Lower values indicate better conditional likelihood under this particular scoring procedure, but these values are not interchangeable with the primary MLM loss or perplexity.

The mean domain-token PLL is $-1.64$ for RoBERTa and $-1.95$ for ModernBERT, while the mean general-token PLL is $-2.59$ for ModernBERT and $-2.83$ for RoBERTa. These diagnostic scores add nuance to the aggregate results by separating behaviour on domain terms from behaviour on general tokens. Taken together, the primary benchmark and the targeted diagnostics favour ModernBERT as a broad initialization while showing why technical-term recovery should be evaluated explicitly during subsequent domain adaptation.

\subsection{Initialization Decision}
\label{sec:decision}

ModernBERT-base is selected as the primary initialization for domain-adaptive pretraining (DAPT). The decision rests on converging evidence: it achieves the highest primary mean Top-1 accuracy at 57.99\%, has a MECS of 87.40, remains the leading model across the evaluated matched conditions, and is non-dominated in the multi-criteria analysis. It also shows 100\% bootstrap rank-1 stability and no statistically significant pairwise defeats after Holm correction. Its 20 wins in 25 conditions against RoBERTa demonstrate that its advantage is strong but not universal across every configuration.

BERT-base remains a reasonable alternative when operational constraints take priority. It uses 110M parameters and has a measured latency of 19.51\,ms, although its primary mean Top-1 accuracy is lower. RoBERTa is the closest alternative on the primary predictive metric, while SciBERT and Legal-BERT remain non-dominated under the selected multi-objective criteria. The DA-Rare findings further indicate that the adaptation process should monitor rare maritime terminology rather than assuming that a high aggregate score guarantees strong performance on every technical subset.

The scope of this decision is the selection of an initialization under the present intrinsic benchmark. Masked-token recovery and sampled pseudo-log-likelihood evaluate predictive behaviour on held-out text; they do not by themselves establish improved named-entity recognition, incident classification, retrieval, or operational decision support. ModernBERT is therefore the best-supported starting point among the evaluated candidates, while its practical value must be established through subsequent DAPT validation and downstream task evaluation.
